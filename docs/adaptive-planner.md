# Adaptive study planning

The Study Plan page builds a rolling seven-day schedule from explicit availability and recorded preparation evidence. The scheduler is deterministic and runs without OpenAI. Nothing is seeded as completed, and no generated session writes an attempt, a story result, or a readiness score.

The availability editor has five tabs: **Study schedule**, **Interview rounds**, **Days off & exceptions**, **Advanced settings**, and **AI coaching**. Session length, breaks, and preparation phase boundaries are in Advanced settings. The footer keeps Save and Cancel visible while the form body scrolls. Left/Right arrows, Home, and End move between tabs; drafts persist when switching sections. See [the UX review and screenshots](planner-ux-review.md).

The weekly calendar labels available windows separately from the daily study limit. Planned minutes include completed session estimates; remaining minutes count study sessions still planned. Completed counts do not establish recorded duration or correctness. Detailed evidence and time-accounting explanations remain available under **Why this plan?**.

## Enable locally

1. Keep your existing database and apply only `supabase/migrations/202610180001_adaptive_planner.sql` manually in the Supabase SQL editor. Do not rerun or edit applied migrations. The migration adds `planner_state`, `study_plans`, and `planner_coach_requests`, owner RLS, validated RPCs, and revision triggers on existing evidence tables. It does not backfill or rewrite preparation history.
2. Configure `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` as before. No service-role key is used.
3. Open **Study plan → Edit availability**. Enter the study timezone, exact interview rounds and interview timezone, daily study-hour cap, recurring time windows, optional date overrides, unavailable periods, session length, breaks, and intensity. Empty windows mean unavailable. An empty override is a day off. The daily cap counts focused study; breaks also consume real window time.
4. Save. A baseline assessment and a real-experience behavioral task appear when no coding history exists. With evidence, the same schedule adapts automatically. Unknown durations remain unknown.

Legacy date-only interviews remain supported conservatively: that interview day is unavailable until an exact round is supplied. No interview time is guessed. Round times that fall in a DST gap are rejected; an ambiguous repeated time uses its earlier occurrence consistently in JavaScript and PostgreSQL. Overnight availability must be split across adjacent days. `24:00` is supported as a window end in the stored contract; a native form end time of `00:00` explicitly means midnight at the end of that day.

## Evidence and priority

`src/lib/adaptive-planner.ts` reuses the original planner's date arithmetic and baseline/pattern fallback. The scheduler and its time utilities take an explicit current instant; they never read a clock, access the network, or mutate source data.

- Latest outcomes per distinct coding problem determine pattern weakness. A first recorded independent Medium pass has more weight than a familiar repeat. First recorded does **not** establish that a problem was unseen outside LoopPilot. An independent pass requires self-reported `passed`, `independent`, and zero hints. Assistance, failures, repeated hints, and low confidence raise review priority. Sample tests never promote correctness or mastery.
- Reviews start at D+1, D+3, and D+7 from the first attempt. Only independent passes on later distinct eligible days advance the stage. Failed or assisted retries do not advance it; they return no earlier than the next day, gain priority, and can reopen recovery review after the initial stages. Overdue priority grows, with at most five review candidates per day. Task completion has no effect on repetition.
- Behavioral candidates use actual STAR fields, evidence and follow-up presence, recorded confidence/status, and Leadership Principle coverage. Separate rehearsal results are not stored in the current product; they are never invented. Up to two story rehearsals and a missing-principle story candidate compete for time.
- Estimates use recorded timer seconds, then historical minutes, where available. Missing durations use explicitly labeled planning estimates, never invented attempt durations. The daily budget accounts for the maximum of logged session minutes, attempt durations, or estimates for completed planned sessions, reducing obvious double counting. This cannot identify every overlap between independently entered logs. Completion records a timestamp, not an actual study duration.
- Two failures or multiple-hint attempts among the latest three recorded attempts shorten sessions to at most 30 minutes and lower the daily budget by 25%. This is a workload response to recorded struggle, not an inferred fatigue diagnosis. Gentle intensity uses 75% of daily hours; balanced/intensive use the full cap.
- Category diversity reduces repeated category priority after each selection so coding/reviews do not routinely crowd out behavioral work. The scheduler may leave unused time when suitable candidates run out or gaps are too short. It never fills hours with fictitious work or assumed future successes.
- Existing manually scheduled tasks remain intact and enter the candidate queue. Their completion remains separate from generated session completion. Saved readiness snapshots are contextual evidence only, not proof of mastery or offer probabilities.

The next unexpired round and its format guide strategy. Defaults are foundations above 21 days, focused independent/timed work at 8–21 days, interview rehearsal at 3–7 days, and light review in the final 48 hours. Boundaries are configurable. Recorded struggle and baseline needs can override the general phase mix. Light review caps focused work at 90 minutes and avoids new coding topics. Mock sessions are explicitly **self-directed rehearsal**; there is no mock-scoring backend, external coach, or fabricated result. No new sessions follow an interview on its local study date. Later rounds guide subsequent days.

## Lifecycle and persistence

`use-adaptive-planner.ts` runs throughout the authenticated workspace. It checks revisions after local CRUD refreshes (including IDE saves), settings/control changes, focus/visibility changes, and once per minute. The minute check notices new local dates, elapsed sessions, and changes made from another tab/device. Unchanged revisions and a complete seven-day horizon reuse stored plans; they do not regenerate or call AI on keystrokes. The Regenerate button explicitly recalculates remaining sessions.

Planning runs while the app is open and on reopening. There is no cron job generating plans while the app is closed. A newly opened app catches up immediately. Future days are provisional: a saved attempt changes tomorrow's plan, while future improvement is never assumed.

Each recommendation has a stable date-specific key. `study_plans` has an account/date primary key; repeated commits update that day's plan rather than append duplicate tasks. Evidence triggers increment an account revision. Writes lock the account state and compare the expected revision; the client retries one stale-generation conflict from fresh data. Atomic commits validate references, real local availability, unavailable periods, interview blocks, intervals, overlap and breaks in PostgreSQL. Authenticated clients have SELECT privileges on planner tables and mutate them through owner-checked RPCs with an empty search path.

Completed, skipped, missed, started, and pinned items survive regeneration. A deliberate skip/completion/pin/reschedule is a separate control action. Elapsed planned sessions become missed, never completed. Early completion can free wall-clock room, while the completed session's estimated minutes still count against the study budget. Pinned sessions that conflict after availability/timezone changes are retained and visibly flagged; the planner places no additional overlapping work. The user must resolve such a conflict by rescheduling, skipping, or unpinning it. Historical plan rows are retained.

Rescheduling fits an available free interval and pins the result. Moving across dates in the generated week keeps the source session as skipped and moves the same recommendation to the target day; protected target sessions cannot be replaced. Calendar synchronization is not implemented. UTC intervals and pure availability subtraction provide the boundary for a later calendar adapter.

## Optional OpenAI coaching

Choose the AI provider explicitly in Settings: rule-based (default), eligible ChatGPT plan, or OpenAI API (separately billed). Enable local settings with server-only `LOOPPILOT_CHATGPT_LOCAL=1` and the exact `LOOPPILOT_LOCAL_ORIGIN=http://127.0.0.1:3000`. ChatGPT uses the official OAuth connection and an account-discovered model, without an API key. Only the API provider uses server-only `OPENAI_API_KEY` and optionally `OPENAI_PLANNER_MODEL` (default `gpt-4o-mini`). Never use a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` variable for an OpenAI secret. Enable the separate consent checkbox in availability preferences only if you want OpenAI to receive a structured performance summary. No real external AI call was made during implementation or tests. See [local setup, OAuth, security, and verification instructions](../README.md#use-your-chatgpt-plan-locally).

The authenticated `/api/planner/coach` route verifies the Supabase bearer token and reads authoritative stored evidence using the caller's RLS context. It sends counts, pattern summaries, story coverage/completeness booleans, confidence, and scheduled session identifiers/categories/durations. It excludes solution code, story narrative, notes, names, and email. Both providers set `store: false`. The API-key request retains its 20-second timeout and output limit. ChatGPT uses the official streaming Responses endpoint, array input, a 60-second timeout, and no unsupported preview fields; only `response.completed` with completed status can be accepted. See the [official Structured Outputs documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs?api-mode=responses) and [ChatGPT preview contract](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source/preview-limitations).

AI selects up to three existing focus sessions, a practice approach, a difficulty suggestion, and pacing through strict structured output. Zod and evidence checks reject unknown identifiers, unsupported claims of failure, incompatible phase choices, extra fields, and unsuitable difficulty suggestions. The app renders a short note from validated choices and actual recommendation reasons. Free-form factual claims and AI-supplied schedules are deliberately not accepted. Time allocation, deadlines, eligibility, and the actual plan remain deterministic.

Durable database reservations allow at most five new requests per account per UTC day, at least one minute apart, including failures/in-flight requests. A hash of the provider, ChatGPT registration when applicable, model, and structured evidence caches validated choices for 12 hours. A failed reservation is not retried for the same evidence that day; a later meaningful change may produce a new key within the quota. Key absence, refusal, timeout, invalid output, stale evidence, or quota exhaustion shows a clear deterministic fallback. Provider/account/model selection and evidence revision are rechecked before caching completed choices. Requests never switch billing providers. The UI labels ChatGPT/API provider, model, and cached status. Cached choices contain no raw history or secret keys.
